Prompt

Design a way for users to relate to time using audio. This relation does not have to be quantitative measurement.


Context

Clocks of various types are usually the instrument most strongly related to measuring regular intervals of time such as seconds, minutes and hours. Audio may instead be used to measure different intervals or otherwise change a user's experience of time, such as breaths in a period or meditation, or hold music while waiting on the phone.


Discussion

My interpretation of the brief is that it’s focused about how we understand and perceive time, and how this can be explored and measured through audio rather than typical instruments like the clock. Audio can have such a strong impact on one’s emotions and mood, so being able to harness this can help us form a deeper connection with time. I think that being able to auralise time can positively impact a person’s experience with it.


The brief allows me to think about different ways of measuring time whether it be quantitative or something else that’s less conventional. I can envision how both of these forms could be transformed with audio to relate to people, whether it be bringing more insight to an already developed measure of time, or by focusing on aspects of time that aren’t as familiarly represented. Either way, I believe that the tool will be used less like a typical instrument would, to make music, and more as a way to give personal meaning to something. The tool shouldn’t need much technical experience and should be more focused on providing the user with a better/more connected understanding of the chosen concept of time.


The three main values behind the brief I have identified are personality, connection, and enlightenment. The tool should have personality in the form of what it’s sound conveys to the user and how the user interacts with the sound. It should give the user a better sense of the different parts of the tool by attributing emotions to them, provoking different feelings. This comes in partnership with the value of connection, giving users a more intimate relationship with the project and its features. Being able to have a mutual understanding with the program makes it so much more powerful, so having ways to personally connect with it and customise it will be an important design consideration. Since we are working with time, I think that the audio aspect should help to convey something to make it feel more meaningful. It should enlighten the user either by providing useful information or giving a better sensation for how the time is interacting with you.


Synopsis

My idea is to design a personal calendar that implements audio to showcase the user’s day/week/month/etc. as a sonic story. It will include personalisation features and time adapting sound effects to create an immersive experience.


Response to purpose and worth

The calendar responds to the purpose of giving a concept of time a deepened meaning. It does this by giving the user an authentic sonic perspective on the traditional calendar. While we are mostly working with quantitative time on a calendar with the use of hours, days, weeks, etc, there is also the element of time “vibes” or positive/negative time, like how nighttime and daytime feel different to different people. Utilising this, the program will be able to tell a story with sound depending on these different variables. The user will be able to customise what sound the calendar makes, depending on the activity. This customisation will be more feeling based and not require any prior technical knowledge to use. This is because I want the project to be more focused on the user’s emotions and how they perceive their calendar, rather than their musical abilities and complex sound knowledge. The project will be able to give the user different ways of approaching these sounds whether it be in the form of a captivating notification or an insightful musical adventure of the user’s upcoming week.


Personality is addressed through giving the calendar its own distinct sonic “voice”. Rather than just having some boring notification chime, the tool builds its own world of sounds unique to the user, with different activities playing different sounds. The user has control over which sounds are used and can match up the feelings they give, making the experience feel more personal.


Connection to the calendar builds on this by relating its tasks and reminders to the real world. It gives the user a sense of understanding and creates a bond between themselves and the sounds. This might be achieved by differing the sounds depending on the time of day, number of activities, length of activity, and more.


Enlightenment in my project will take the form of giving the user a new perspective of their own life. The use of audio guides the user to better recognise patterns and gain a more in-depth outlook on how their day-to-day functions. Users can also gain insight of fun or stressful activities by learning which sounds evoke positive or negative feelings.


Project Framing

The core conceptual idea of my project is treating the calendar more as a storytelling object rather than a record-keeping one. While it should still have a lot of the normal calendar functionalities, the emphasis is more on how it uses sound to connect with the user in an emotional rather than technical way. This concept can be seen through musician Brian Eno’s works, where he has composed his music to be experienced passively, evoking atmosphere and mood, rather than something that demands musical focus.


I want the final product to be playful and charming to help bridge the gap between the seriousness of an ordinary calendar. I also want to give it mindful and almost relaxing characteristics to bring a feeling of serenity when interacting with it so that users can connect to it without feeling overwhelmed. It’s not as important to me to make the calendar professional as that’s not the main focus of the project, however I may choose to include it as an added option or theme to give users more control and help them with finding familiarity in the tool if they need it.


The project will be fully coded in VSCode in the form of a website using HTML, CSS and JavaScript, and uploaded to the web via GitHub Pages. I will also be using the free JavaScript audio framework, Tone.js, to create the interactive sounds in the browser, employing its ability to change frequency, amplitude, tone, etc. I will make use of JavaScript’s in-built date and time objects to help set-up the calendar and translate some of its information into variables that can be used to create the different sounds. There may be uses for other extended techniques in my project such as being able to use location settings to change calendar activities/sounds or possibly using randomness to help users create sounds that are new and refreshing. It would even be reasonable to use JavaScript’s spatial input commands maybe as a way to play sounds on hovering different parts of the calendar. My current thoughts for customisation are to use built-in sliders that change the sounds based on an emotion or feeling to help keep the UI simple and easy to use.


Interaction Design Analysis

Example 1: Dialed.gg

This example showcases a great way of handling characterisation. As soon as you load into the website you are greeted with a simple, rounded black and white design.



The benefit of this is it lets its sound design do the talking as when you hover over each item an appealing little pop or hum plays. Depending on your chosen game mode some elements of the sounds change too for example: the sound game has a low humming noise to emulate a deep soundwave. Everything comes together to create a soft and inquisitive design giving users the feeling of satisfaction. I would like to adapt this characterisation into my project by using these rounded edges and simplistic designs to create a serene environment.


Example 2: Got Rhythm?

This similar example also brings a gamified experience to sound but in a more rhythmic way. The overall design and sound design of this site is a lot less polished in my opinion and the responsiveness and UI feels clunky. The way that the information is presented feels incohesive as the drumbeat sound feels quite harsh and comes out of nowhere and the shapes don’t have a meaningful relationship with the user’s clicks. The information in the graph at the end does provide useful insight but by the time I get there I feel like I’ve been left a bit unsettled.



In fairness to the website, it is more centred around being a tool to test and learn about staying on beat rather than sound itself but there is much to learn from its overall design. I will improve my own project from this by trying to limit its clunkiness and making sure that the buttons and sounds all flow together without feel abrupt or disjointed.


Example 3: 4four

I chose this example to compare its purpose with mine. While our programs will be vastly different, what this program does well that I can learn from is its education for the user. The program does not look spectacular by any means, it efficiently solves its problem case. With multiple different instruments to choose from, the website serves as a hub for many different people wanting to achieve the same goal in different ways. When playing each instrument, the tool visually tells you which note you are playing which gives the user fantastic insight into how they can improve.



They also utilise sliders as a simple way to control volume and bpm. I think there is a lot to learn from this type of design and how it displays its information to the user to achieve its main purpose. This has provoked me to think about how I might want to add an easier way for user to learn the information from the calendar, maybe by adding in visual cues too.


Example 4: Google Calendar

Finally, we have Google calendar. The values for this app are quite simplistic with the with focuses on being organised, accessible, and to be honest, boring. While this may work for a traditional calendar, it creates a very rigid and unforgiving vibe to the overall site



It becomes easy to dehumanise this scheduling process which is something that I want to bring back with my project. While I may use similar layouts to format my calendar, I want to branch away from this corporate style and make the calendar process feel like less of a chore. My goal is to implement more “feel” based inputs to bring the calendar alive by diverging from this basic, blocky list.
